Phase 1 — Switch ON

Vx snaps to +48V (Vin connects directly across L to ground). Current ramps up, storing energy in the inductor. The diode is reverse-biased, so the output capacitor alone supplies the load.

Phase 2 — Switch OFF

The inductor's current can't stop instantly, so it pulls Vx negative until the diode conducts — Vx settles at −Vout. Current ramps down as stored energy flows out through D into the load, with reversed polarity relative to Vin.
The switching-node voltage here is bipolar (swings positive then negative) — a signature of the inverting buck-boost topology, unlike the buck (0/Vin) or boost (0/Vout) cases. Its average is always zero in steady state.
