Phase 1 — Switch ON (primary energizing)

Vin drives current through the primary winding only. Energy builds up as a magnetic field in the core. Because of the winding polarity (dot convention), the secondary diode is reverse-biased — no current flows on the secondary side, and no energy crosses to the output yet. The capacitor alone supplies the load.

Phase 2 — Switch OFF (secondary discharging)

The primary current is cut off. The core's stored energy reverses the secondary winding's voltage, forward-biasing the diode — current now flows only on the secondary side, transferring the stored energy to the output capacitor and load. Primary and secondary current never flow at the same time.
The dashed green line is the isolation barrier — there is no direct wire between primary and secondary; all energy crosses the core magnetically. This is what lets the output float at a different voltage/ground reference than the input.
