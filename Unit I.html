Typical Robotic Power Architecture

Energy Source

The battery stores energy and supplies the electrical power required by the robot.

Power Conversion

DC-DC converters and inverters convert voltage, current and frequency into suitable forms.

Control

Digital controllers regulate voltage, current, speed, torque and position.

Protection

Protection includes over-current, over-voltage, under-voltage, short-circuit and thermal protection.

Battery Types and Power Requirements

The battery is one of the most important components in a mobile robot because it determines operating time, weight, peak power capability and mobility.

Lithium-Ion

High energy density and widely used in mobile robots.

Li-Po

High discharge capability and lightweight construction.

LiFePO₄

Good safety, long cycle life and thermal stability.

Lead Acid

Low cost but high weight and lower energy density.

Example: A 24 V, 20 Ah battery stores approximately:
E = 24 × 20 = 480 Wh
If a robot consumes 240 W continuously, the ideal operating time is:
t = 480 / 240 = 2 hours
Actual operating time will be lower because of converter losses, motor losses, battery characteristics and changing load.

Power Switching Devices – MOSFET and IGBT

Power electronic converters use semiconductor switches to rapidly turn electrical power ON and OFF.

MOSFET

MOSFET
  • Voltage-controlled device
  • Very fast switching
  • Low gate power
  • Excellent for high-frequency converters
  • Common in low/medium voltage applications

IGBT

IGBT
  • Voltage-controlled gate
  • Higher voltage capability
  • Lower conduction loss at high voltage/current
  • Slower than MOSFET
  • Common in motor drives and inverters

DC Bus Architecture in Robotics

The DC bus acts as a common electrical energy distribution point between the battery and different power converters.

Functions of the DC Bus

Energy Distribution

Provides a common voltage source for multiple converter stages.

Voltage Stabilization

DC-link capacitors reduce voltage fluctuations and high-frequency ripple.

Energy Buffer

The DC-link capacitor temporarily stores electrical energy during rapid load changes.

Regeneration

With a bidirectional converter, braking energy can be returned to the battery.

Converter Selection for Robotic Applications

The converter should not be selected only based on voltage. Current, power, efficiency, switching frequency, isolation, thermal performance, EMI and control requirements must also be considered.

1. Input Voltage

Determine minimum, nominal and maximum input voltage.

2. Output Voltage

Determine the required voltage range for the load.

3. Power Rating

Calculate continuous and peak load power.

4. Efficiency

Higher efficiency reduces battery energy consumption and heat.

5. Isolation

Determine whether galvanic isolation is required.

6. Switching Frequency

Higher frequency reduces passive component size but may increase switching losses and EMI.

Important Buck Converter Relation

Vo = D Vin

D = Vo / Vin
Example: For a 24 V input and 12 V output:
D = 12 / 24 = 0.5
Therefore the ideal duty ratio is 50%.

Isolation in Robotic Power Systems

Galvanic isolation electrically separates the input and output circuits while transferring energy magnetically or optically.

Isolated DC-DC Converter Concept

Why Isolation is Used

  • Electrical safety
  • Ground-loop prevention
  • Protection of sensitive electronics
  • Noise reduction
  • Different ground domains
  • Industrial robot power supplies
Isolation generally increases converter complexity, size and cost. The transformer and insulation must be designed for the required voltage, frequency and safety requirements.

EMI / EMC Considerations in Robotic Systems

Fast switching devices generate rapidly changing voltage and current. These transitions can produce electromagnetic interference.

EMI Generation Mechanism

Conducted and Radiated EMI

Conducted EMI

Noise travels through electrical conductors such as DC power cables, signal grounds and supply rails.

Radiated EMI

High-frequency currents and voltage transitions can create electromagnetic fields that couple to nearby circuits.

EMI Reduction Techniques

Technique Purpose
EMI Filter Attenuates conducted high-frequency noise
Snubber Reduces voltage spikes and ringing
Gate Resistor Controls switching speed
Dead Time Prevents shoot-through
Proper Grounding Reduces unwanted current paths
Short PCB Loops Reduces parasitic inductance
Shielding Reduces radiated coupling
Important: High switching frequency can reduce passive component size, but it can also increase switching losses and EMI. Therefore, converter design is always a compromise between size, efficiency, thermal performance and electromagnetic compatibility.
